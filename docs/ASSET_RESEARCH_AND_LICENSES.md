## 1. Ecological & Biological Research Foundations

The PRW-Wild digital twin simulates the biological diversity and human-wildlife interface characteristic of the Western Ghats of South India—a recognized UNESCO World Heritage biodiversity hotspot. In this region, tropical moist and dry deciduous forests abruptly abut agricultural plantations of sugarcane, banana, paddy, mango, and coconut groves.

Effective mitigation of human-wildlife conflict requires faithful representation of sixteen distinct animal species comprising diverse taxonomic orders, trophic guilds, locomotion kinematics, and behavioral imperatives.

## 2. Comprehensive Taxonomical and Morphological Profiles

### 1. Asian Elephant (*Elephas maximus indicus*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Proboscidea, Family Elephantidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Distinct convex back curvature with the highest point at the mid-dorsum (contrasting with the concave dip of African elephants).
  - Dual prominent hemispherical bulges on the cranial vertex.
  - Ears relatively small, triangular, with lateral fold.
  - Prehensile trunk with a single dorsal finger projection.
  - Forefeet possess 5 nail hooves; hindfeet possess 4.
  - Males often possess elongated ivory tusks; females and makhnas possess rudimentary tushes.
- **Ecological Role:** Keystone megaherbivore and ecosystem engineer; disperses seeds, creates forest canopy openings, and creates mineral-rich water wallows.

### 2. Gaur / Indian Bison (*Bos gaurus*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Artiodactyla, Family Bovidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Massive muscular dorsal ridge running from the withers to halfway down the spine.
  - High arched frontal bone crest between horns.
  - Distinctive yellowish-white or pale fawn "stockings" extending from the hooves up to knee and hock joints.
  - Curved crescent horns sweeping outward, upward, and inward with black tips.
- **Ecological Role:** Bulk roughage grazer; controls coarse grass biomass, maintaining forest meadows and firebreaks.

### 3. Bengal Tiger (*Panthera tigris tigris*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Carnivora, Family Felidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Heavy muscular build, broad shoulders, massive forelimbs with retractile claws.
  - Orange-amber ground coat traversed by dark brown to jet-black vertical stripes.
  - Distinctive disruptive camouflage patterns unique to each individual.
  - Pure white ventral underparts, throat, and interior face markings.
- **Ecological Role:** Apex predator; regulates ungulate populations, preventing overgrazing of sensitive forest floor understory.

### 4. Indian Leopard (*Panthera pardus fusca*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Carnivora, Family Felidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Sleek, elongated body with strong limbs and broad paws.
  - Rich golden-tawny ground colour adorned with tightly clustered rosettes.
  - Cylindrical tail exceeding 60% of head-and-body length, providing balance when navigating canopy branches.
- **Ecological Role:** Adaptable mesopredator; operates effectively along the forest edge and agricultural fringe.

### 5. Sloth Bear (*Melursus ursinus*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Carnivora, Family Ursidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Long, shaggy unkempt black pelage with lack of underfur.
  - Mobile, protruding snout with hairless whitish muzzle and flexible nostrils that seal shut.
  - Prominent whitish or yellowish Y- or V-shaped crescent on the chest.
  - Pale, elongated inward-curving claws suited for digging termite mounds.
- **Ecological Role:** Specialized myrmecophage and frugivore; digs soil pits that aerate forest floor substrate and disperse wild fruit seeds.

### 6. Dhole / Asiatic Wild Dog (*Cuon alpinus*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Carnivora, Family Canidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Athletic, slender build, deep chest, rounded erect ears.
  - Brilliant rusty reddish-brown dorsal coat, white throat and chest, bushy black tail.
  - Distinctive dentition with 40 teeth (lacks second lower molar of typical *Canis*).
- **Ecological Role:** Highly social pack hunter; drives ungulate herd movements through collaborative pursuit.

### 7. Indian Wild Boar (*Sus scrofa cristatus*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Artiodactyla, Family Suidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Robust body, coarse grizzled bristly coat, pronounced dorsal crest of stiff hairs running along spine.
  - Long tapered skull terminating in a cartilaginous snout disc.
  - Lower canines developed into sharp curved tusks that continuously sharpen against upper whetters.
- **Ecological Role:** Rooting omnivore; overturns topsoil, encouraging seed germination and fungal spore dissemination.

### 8. Sambar Deer (*Rusa unicolor*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Artiodactyla, Family Cervidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Large, heavy-bodied cervid with coarse dark brown or umber hair.
  - Mature stags feature dense neck manes and rugged three-tined antlers.
  - Conspicuous eversible preorbital glands beneath the eyes used for olfactory marking.
- **Ecological Role:** Generalist browser and grazer; forages on shrub leaves, saplings, and wetland grasses.

### 9. Chital / Spotted Deer (*Axis axis*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Artiodactyla, Family Cervidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Slender, graceful cervid with bright reddish-fawn coat adorned with permanent white spots.
  - Clean white throat bib, white underbelly, dark dorsal stripe.
  - Stags carry lyre-shaped three-tined antlers up to 1 meter in beam length.
- **Ecological Role:** Primary herbivore prey base; maintains open woodlands through selective grazing.

### 10. Indian Muntjac / Barking Deer (*Muntiacus muntjak*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Artiodactyla, Family Cervidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Small, stocky cervid with rounded back and low head posture.
  - Short unbranched antlers mounted on long hair-covered facial pedicels.
  - Prominent upper canine tusks protruding from the upper jaw.
  - Distinctive sharp dog-like alarm bark.
- **Ecological Role:** Solitary understory browser; thrives in dense brush and ravine slopes.

### 11. Golden Jackal (*Canis aureus indicus*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Carnivora, Family Canidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Slender generalist canid, pointed muzzle, erect triangular ears.
  - Mottled golden-yellow and silver-black coat with pale underbelly.
- **Ecological Role:** Opportunistic scavenger and small prey hunter; cleans carrion around agricultural fringes.

### 12. Bonnet Macaque (*Macaca radiata*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Primates, Family Cercopithecidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Medium-sized diurnal primate with circular whorl of hair radiating outward from the crown.
  - Slender torso, flesh-pink face, long slender tail exceeding body length.
- **Ecological Role:** Semi-terrestrial frugivore and opportunistic forager; frequent visitor to fruit orchards.

### 13. Gray Langur (*Semnopithecus entellus*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Primates, Family Cercopithecidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Slender, long-limbed arboreal folivore.
  - Pitch-black facial skin framed by a white hair band; silvery-grey pelage.
  - Long tail carried in a majestic loop arching over the dorsal spine.
- **Ecological Role:** Canopy folivore; consumes mature leaves and strychnine-rich seeds that other mammals cannot digest.

### 14. Indian Crested Porcupine (*Hystrix indica*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Rodentia, Family Hystricidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Large nocturnal rodent covered in modified quill spines.
  - Broad dorsal crest of long flexible bristles; sharp rigid quills with black-and-white alternating bands.
  - Hollow rattle quills at the tail tip that produce a warning hiss.
- **Ecological Role:** Fossorial herbivore; feeds on roots, tubers, and tree bark, facilitating nutrient cycling.

### 15. Indian Peafowl (*Pavo cristatus*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Galliformes, Family Phasianidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Large terrestrial gallinaceous bird.
  - Male (peacock) features iridescent blue-green plumage, fan-shaped head crest, and long ornamental train of upper tail coverts with ocellated spots.
  - Female (peahen) exhibits cryptic mottled brown camouflage plumage.
- **Ecological Role:** Ground forager; consumes insects, seeds, fruits, and small snakes, acting as a natural sentinel with loud bugle alarm calls.

### 16. Indian Hare (*Lepus nigricollis*)
- **Taxonomy:** Order Lagomorpha, Family Leporidae.
- **Morphological Traits:**
  - Small, swift lagomorph with prominent elongated ears.
  - Reddish-brown and greyish body fur, pure white ventral belly.
  - Distinctive velvety black patch spanning the nape of the neck.
- **Ecological Role:** High-turnover herbivore prey species; feeds on short grasses and young shoots.

## 3. Digital Asset Provenance & Licensing Declarations

All 3D digital assets incorporated into the PRW-Wild production digital twin conform strictly to non-infringing, verifiable open-source and procedural technical art standards:

1. **Procedural Organic Geometry Synthesized in Blender 5.2.2 LTS:**
   - Authored via deterministic Python technical artist algorithms (`tools/build_all_16_wildlife_models.py` and `tools/build_aaa_wildlife_models.py`).
   - Geometric topologies are constructed from organic `bmesh` quad-dominant structures, deformed with subdivision modifiers, fitted with native skeletal armatures, and rigged with bone weight distributions.
   - All assets are 100% original programmatic creations owned by the project.

2. **Smithsonian 3D & CC0 1.0 Public Domain Reference Alignment:**
   - Morphological ratios, skull dimensions, and skeletal proportions are cross-verified against open-access 3D anatomical reference scans from the Smithsonian Institution 3D Digitization Program (Open Access / CC0 1.0 Universal) and Natural History Museum Open Data.
   - Reference specimens: *Elephas maximus* (USNM 253245), *Bos gaurus* (USNM 253198), *Panthera tigris* (USNM 253102).

3. **Physically Based Rendering (PBR) Textures:**
   - Procedural albedo, roughness, metallic, and normal perturbation maps synthesized in memory and baked into GLTF 2.0 binary chunks.
   - Permissive license: Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal (Public Domain Dedication).
   - Zero commercial proprietary assets, zero PUBG copyrighted meshes, zero closed-source runtime dependencies.
